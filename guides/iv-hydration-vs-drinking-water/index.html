IV Hydration vs. Drinking Water: What's the Difference?

When drinking is enough, and when an IV makes sense.

By the Optimized Performance & Wellness nursing team · Updated November 2026

Quick answer

Drinking water is enough for everyday hydration. IV hydration delivers fluid and electrolytes directly into your bloodstream, bypassing the stomach, so it works faster and helps when you're nauseated, vomiting, significantly dehydrated or need to recover quickly. An IV also lets you add vitamins and medications when appropriate.

The short version

When drinking water is enough

For most healthy people on a normal day, drinking water and eating normally keeps you well hydrated. You don't need an IV to stay hydrated day to day.

When IV hydration makes sense

  • You're sick and can't keep fluids down
  • You've lost a lot of fluid from fever, vomiting, diarrhea or heavy sweating
  • You're recovering from a race, tournament or long day in the South Florida heat
  • You want to rehydrate before or after travel
  • You want vitamins like vitamin C, B12 or glutathione delivered with your fluids

What's in our IV fluids

Every drip starts with 1 liter of Lactated Ringer's or normal saline. These are sterile solutions with electrolytes that closely match your body's own fluids. See our ingredient guide for everything else we add.
