About IV therapy

What is mobile IV therapy?

Mobile IV therapy means a licensed nurse comes to your home, office or hotel and gives you IV fluids, vitamins and, when appropriate, medications through a small IV in your arm. You get the same kind of IV hydration you'd get in a clinic without driving anywhere or sitting in a waiting room.

Does an IV actually help when I'm sick?

When you're sick it's easy to get dehydrated, especially with fever, vomiting or not eating. IV fluids rehydrate you faster than sipping water, and many people feel noticeably better after. It supports your recovery, it doesn't cure the illness.

Is IV hydration better than drinking water?

For everyday hydration, drinking water is usually enough. IV fluids go directly into your bloodstream, so they're helpful when you're too nauseated to keep fluids down, significantly dehydrated or need to rehydrate quickly. Read our full comparison.

What's in an IV drip?

Every drip starts with 1 liter of Lactated Ringer's or normal saline. We then add vitamins, minerals, antioxidants or amino acids depending on the drip, such as vitamin C, glutathione, B12, zinc or magnesium. See every ingredient in our ingredient guide.

How long does a drip take?

About 45 to 60 minutes from setup to finish.

Where do you go?

Homes, offices, hotels and rentals across northern Palm Beach County and the Treasure Coast: Jupiter, Tequesta, Juno Beach, Palm Beach Gardens, North Palm Beach, Hobe Sound, Stuart and West Palm Beach. Don't see your town? Text us, we'll likely still come.

Can you treat the whole family?

Yes, we treat ages 10 and older. Let us know how many people when you text so your nurse brings enough for everyone.

Does it hurt?

Just a quick pinch when the IV goes in. After that most people barely notice it.

What should I do before my nurse arrives?

Drink some water if you can, have a comfortable place to sit or lie down, and have a list of your medications and allergies handy. Wear a top with sleeves that roll up easily.

Safety + who we are

Who gives the IV?

Every IV is overseen by Barry, our licensed registered nurse. Every visit starts with a health screening to make sure your drip is safe and right for you.

Who oversees your treatments?

Our licensed nurses are overseen by a medical director. Telehealth visits with our primary care provider are also available to schedule for a fee.

Is IV therapy safe?

IV therapy is generally well tolerated when it's given by a licensed professional after a health screening. Your nurse reviews your health history, medications and allergies before every drip and won't proceed if an IV isn't right for you. Read about our safety process.

Are the Get Right meds required?

No. They're optional and only given when appropriate after your nurse's assessment.

When should I go to a doctor or ER instead?

IV hydration isn't emergency care. For trouble breathing, chest pain, confusion, a fever that won't come down, or severe dehydration in a child, call your doctor or 911.
