Safety + health screening

Every single drip starts with a health screening by a licensed nurse. Here's what we check and why.

How we keep you safe

Every IV from Optimized Performance & Wellness is given by a licensed nurse, overseen by a medical director, after a health screening that reviews your symptoms, health history, medications, allergies and vital signs. If an IV isn't right for you, your nurse won't proceed. IV hydration isn't emergency care.

What your nurse checks

  • Your symptoms and how long you've had them
  • Your health history, including heart, kidney and other conditions
  • Medications you take and any allergies
  • Vital signs
  • Whether you're pregnant or could be

Medications are optional and assessed

Medications like Toradol, Zofran, Pepcid and Benadryl are never automatic. They're optional and only given when appropriate after your nurse's assessment.

Ages we treat

We treat ages 10 and older.

When to call a doctor or 911 instead

IV hydration isn't emergency care. For trouble breathing, chest pain, confusion, a fever that won't come down, or severe dehydration in a child, call your doctor or 911.

Safety FAQ

Is IV therapy safe?

IV therapy is generally well tolerated when it's given by a licensed professional after a health screening. Your nurse reviews your health history, medications and allergies before every drip and won't proceed if an IV isn't right for you. Read about our safety process.

Who gives the IV?

Every IV is overseen by Barry, our licensed registered nurse. Every visit starts with a health screening to make sure your drip is safe and right for you.

When should I go to a doctor or ER instead?

IV hydration isn't emergency care. For trouble breathing, chest pain, confusion, a fever that won't come down, or severe dehydration in a child, call your doctor or 911.

Who might not be a good fit for IV therapy?

Your nurse will ask about conditions that can affect how your body handles fluids or certain ingredients, such as heart or kidney conditions, pregnancy, allergies and your current medications. If an IV isn't right for you, your nurse will tell you and may recommend you see your doctor.

What are the side effects of IV therapy?

Most people only notice a quick pinch. Some may have minor bruising or tenderness at the IV site, or feel a cool sensation as fluids run. Your nurse monitors you during the drip. Contact your doctor if redness, swelling or pain at the site gets worse.
